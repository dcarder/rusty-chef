Dublin Coddle

By Daniel Carder · Dinner · 75 minutes · Irish

Jump to recipe

Coddle is a Dublin classic, a thrifty one-pot supper of sausages, bacon, potatoes and onions.

Everything gently simmers together until the potatoes are tender and the broth is rich and savory.

Brown the sausages first

Traditional coddle is pale, but browning the sausages and bacon first gives the broth a lot more flavor and makes the dish much more appetizing.

Dublin Coddle

  • Prep15 min
  • Cook60 min
  • Total75 min
  • Serves6
  • Est. cost$1.90/serving

Ingredients

  • 6 slices bacon, chopped
  • 1 lb pork sausage links
  • 2 onions, thinly sliced
  • 2 1/2 lbs potatoes, peeled and sliced 1/2 inch thick
  • 2 carrots, sliced (optional)
  • 3 cups chicken stock
  • 1 teaspoon dried thyme
  • Salt and pepper
  • Chopped parsley, for serving (optional)

Instructions

  1. Cook the bacon in a large pot or Dutch oven over medium heat for 5 minutes. Move it to a plate.
  2. Brown the sausages in the same pot for 6 to 8 minutes, turning, then move them to the plate and cut each one into thirds.
  3. Layer half the onions and half the potatoes in the pot, then the carrots, sausages and bacon, then the rest of the onions and potatoes. Sprinkle each layer with thyme, salt and pepper.
  4. Pour in the stock. Bring to a boil, then cover and simmer on low for 45 minutes, until the potatoes are very tender.
  5. Uncover for the last 10 minutes to let the broth reduce a little. Top with parsley and serve with soda bread.

Pantry swaps

  • No pork sausage? Any sausage works, including bratwurst or kielbasa.
  • Want it thicker? Mash a few potatoes against the side of the pot at the end.

Rusty Chef tip: Slice the potatoes thick so most hold their shape. The ones on top break down a little and thicken the broth.

Storage

Keeps for 3 days in the fridge. Reheat gently on the stove.
