Easy French Toast

By Daniel Carder · Breakfast · 15 minutes

Jump to recipe

French toast was invented to rescue bread that's past its best, and slightly stale bread actually makes it better.

Dry bread soaks up the egg mixture without falling apart, giving you crisp edges and a soft, custardy middle.

From Daniel's kitchen ♥

I hate wasting food, so when bread starts going stale, this is what happens to it. My wife and kids think it's a special treat, and I think it's a way to save a few bucks. A little cinnamon and syrup and nobody knows it was about to go in the trash.– Daniel

Soak, don't drown

Dip each slice for about 10 seconds per side. Thick or very dry bread can take a little longer. Too long and it gets soggy and won't cook through.

Easy French Toast

  • Prep5 min
  • Cook10 min
  • Total15 min
  • Serves2
  • Est. cost$0.77/serving

Ingredients

  • 4 slices bread, preferably a day old
  • 2 eggs
  • 1/2 cup milk
  • 1/2 teaspoon cinnamon
  • 1 teaspoon sugar
  • 1 tablespoon butter

Instructions

  1. Whisk the eggs, milk, cinnamon and sugar in a shallow dish.
  2. Melt half the butter in a skillet over medium heat.
  3. Dip each slice of bread in the egg mixture for about 10 seconds per side.
  4. Cook for 2 to 3 minutes per side until golden. Add more butter between batches.
  5. Serve with syrup, fruit or a dusting of sugar.

Pantry swaps

  • No cinnamon? A drop of vanilla or a little nutmeg.
  • No milk? Use water with an extra egg.

Rusty Chef tip: Keep the heat at medium. Too hot and the outside burns before the middle cooks.

Storage

Best fresh. Leftovers can be frozen and reheated in a toaster.
