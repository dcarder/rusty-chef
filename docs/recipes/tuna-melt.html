Upgraded Tuna Melt

By Daniel Carder · Lunch · 13 minutes · American

Jump to recipe

A good tuna melt is diner comfort food at its best: creamy tuna salad, gooey cheese and bread fried until golden.

A little mustard and something crunchy and tangy make all the difference.

From Daniel's kitchen ♥

Tuna melts take me straight back to diner lunches. It's one can of tuna and a couple slices of cheese, and it feels like a real meal. My wife and kids like theirs with extra cheese, and I like that lunch costs about a dollar a sandwich.– Daniel

Butter the outside

Spread butter (or mayo) on the outside of the bread before toasting. It browns evenly and gives a crisp, golden crust.

Upgraded Tuna Melt

  • Prep5 min
  • Cook8 min
  • Total13 min
  • Serves2
  • Est. cost$1.43/serving

Ingredients

  • 1 can (5 oz) tuna, drained
  • 2 tablespoons mayonnaise
  • 1 teaspoon mustard
  • 4 slices bread
  • 2 slices cheese
  • 1 tablespoon butter
  • Chopped pickles or onion (optional)

Instructions

  1. Mix the tuna, mayo, mustard and pickles or onion in a bowl.
  2. Butter one side of each slice of bread.
  3. Place two slices butter-side down, top with tuna and cheese, then the remaining bread butter-side up.
  4. Cook in a skillet over medium heat for 3 to 4 minutes per side until golden and melted.
  5. Slice and serve.

Pantry swaps

  • No mayo? Plain yogurt.
  • No tuna? Canned chicken or mashed chickpeas.

Rusty Chef tip: Cover the pan with a lid for the first minute so the cheese melts before the bread burns.

Storage

Tuna salad keeps 2 days in the fridge. Assemble and cook just before eating.
