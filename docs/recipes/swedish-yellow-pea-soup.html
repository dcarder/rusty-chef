Swedish Yellow Pea Soup (Ärtsoppa)

By Daniel Carder · Soup · 85 minutes · Swedish

Jump to recipe

In Sweden, pea soup with ham is a Thursday tradition, usually followed by Swedish pancakes for dessert.

Dried split peas and a little ham turn into a thick, warming soup that costs less than a dollar a bowl.

No soaking needed

Split peas soften without soaking. Just rinse them in a strainer and pick out any tiny stones.

Swedish Yellow Pea Soup (Ärtsoppa)

  • Prep10 min
  • Cook75 min
  • Total85 min
  • Serves6
  • Est. cost$0.95/serving

Ingredients

  • 1 lb dried yellow or green split peas, rinsed
  • 1 tablespoon butter
  • 1 onion, diced
  • 2 carrots, diced (optional)
  • 8 cups water or stock
  • 2 cups diced ham, or 1 ham hock
  • 1 teaspoon dried thyme or marjoram
  • Salt and pepper
  • Mustard, for serving (optional)

Instructions

  1. Melt the butter in a large pot over medium heat. Add the onion and carrots and cook for 5 minutes.
  2. Add the split peas, water or stock, ham and thyme. Bring to a boil and skim off any foam.
  3. Simmer partly covered for 60 to 75 minutes, stirring now and then, until the peas are soft and the soup is thick. Add more water if it gets too thick.
  4. If you used a ham hock, take it out, shred the meat and stir it back in.
  5. Season with salt and pepper. Serve with a spoonful of mustard swirled into each bowl.

Pantry swaps

  • No ham? Use smoked sausage, or make it vegetarian with vegetable stock and 1 teaspoon smoked paprika.
  • Want it smooth? Blend part of the soup with an immersion blender.

Rusty Chef tip: A spoonful of mustard stirred in at the table is the classic Swedish way to eat it. Brown or Dijon mustard both work.

Storage

Keeps for 5 days in the fridge or 3 months in the freezer. It thickens as it sits, so thin it with water when reheating.
