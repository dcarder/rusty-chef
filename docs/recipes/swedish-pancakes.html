Swedish Pancakes

By Daniel Carder · Breakfast · 25 minutes · Swedish

Jump to recipe

Swedish pancakes are thin and delicate like crêpes, with lacy, buttery edges.

In Sweden they're traditionally eaten on Thursdays after pea soup, rolled up with jam and whipped cream.

Let the batter rest

Resting the batter for 10 minutes gives the flour time to soak up the liquid, so the pancakes are tender and less likely to tear.

Swedish Pancakes

  • Prep5 min
  • Cook20 min
  • Total25 min
  • Serves4
  • Est. cost$0.45/serving

Ingredients

  • 3 large eggs
  • 1 1/4 cups milk
  • 3/4 cup flour
  • 1 tablespoon sugar
  • 1/4 teaspoon salt
  • 3 tablespoons butter, melted, plus more for the pan
  • Jam, berries and powdered sugar, for serving (optional)

Instructions

  1. Whisk the eggs with half the milk. Whisk in the flour, sugar and salt until smooth, then whisk in the remaining milk and the melted butter.
  2. Let the batter rest for 10 minutes.
  3. Heat an 8- to 10-inch nonstick skillet over medium heat and brush it with butter.
  4. Pour in 1/4 cup batter and tilt the pan to coat the bottom in a thin layer. Cook for about 1 minute until the edges are golden and lacy, then flip and cook 30 seconds more.
  5. Stack the pancakes on a plate as you go. Spread with jam, roll up and dust with powdered sugar.

Pantry swaps

  • In a hurry? Blend all the batter ingredients in a blender for 20 seconds.
  • No jam? Fill them with sliced bananas, Nutella or a squeeze of lemon and sugar.

Rusty Chef tip: The first pancake is always a tester. Use it to adjust the heat and the amount of batter.

Storage

Stack between parchment and refrigerate for 3 days. Warm in the microwave for a few seconds.
