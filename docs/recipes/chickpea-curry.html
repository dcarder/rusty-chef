6-Ingredient Canned Chickpea Curry

By Daniel Carder · Dinner · 25 minutes

Jump to recipe

This curry proves a few cans and a jar of curry powder can become something you'd happily serve to guests. It's creamy, mildly spiced and on the table in under half an hour.

Everything here keeps for months in the cupboard, which makes it a perfect emergency dinner. Serve it over rice, with flatbread, or on its own in a bowl.

From Daniel's kitchen ♥

I started making this on nights when I was overwhelmed and needed dinner to just happen. Everything comes out of a can, and the house smells amazing in twenty minutes. My wife loved it right away, but the kids were suspicious of the word "curry" at first. Once they tried it over rice, it became a regular in our house.– Daniel

Bloom your spices

The single biggest flavor boost is frying the curry powder in oil for 30 to 60 seconds before adding liquids. Heat wakes up the spice oils, so the curry tastes rich and rounded instead of dusty.

6-Ingredient Canned Chickpea Curry

  • Prep5 min
  • Cook20 min
  • Total25 min
  • Serves4
  • Est. cost$1.34/serving

Ingredients

  • 1 tablespoon oil
  • 1 onion, diced
  • 2 cloves garlic, minced
  • 2 tablespoons curry powder
  • 1 can (15 oz) chickpeas, drained and rinsed
  • 1 can (14 oz) diced tomatoes
  • 1 can (13.5 oz) coconut milk
  • Salt, to taste
  • Handful of spinach (optional)

Instructions

  1. Heat the oil in a large pan over medium heat. Cook the onion for 5 minutes until soft.
  2. Add the garlic and curry powder and stir for 1 minute until fragrant.
  3. Add the chickpeas, tomatoes and coconut milk. Bring to a simmer.
  4. Simmer gently for 12 to 15 minutes, stirring now and then, until thickened.
  5. Stir in the spinach if using, season with salt and serve.

Pantry swaps

  • No coconut milk? Use 1 cup of stock plus 3 tablespoons of plain yogurt stirred in off the heat.
  • No chickpeas? Canned lentils, white beans or cubed potatoes all work.
  • No onion? Use 1 teaspoon onion powder added with the curry powder.

Rusty Chef tip: A squeeze of lemon or a splash of vinegar right before serving brightens the whole pot. If it tastes flat, it usually needs acid or salt.

Storage

Keeps 4 days in the fridge and freezes well for 3 months. It tastes even better the next day.
