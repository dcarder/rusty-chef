Crispy Scallion Pancakes

By Daniel Carder · Snack · 40 minutes

Jump to recipe

Scallion pancakes are a favorite Chinese street food and dim sum snack.

All you need is flour, water, oil and a bunch of green onions.

The coil makes the layers

Rolling the dough up, coiling it into a snail and flattening it again traps oil and green onions in thin layers. That's what makes the pancakes flaky inside and crisp outside.

Crispy Scallion Pancakes

  • Prep25 min
  • Cook15 min
  • Total40 min
  • Serves4
  • Est. cost$0.45/serving

Ingredients

  • 2 cups flour
  • 1 cup water (3/4 cup boiling, 1/4 cup cold)
  • 1/2 teaspoon salt
  • 3 tablespoons oil, plus more for frying
  • 1 teaspoon sesame oil (optional)
  • 1 bunch green onions, thinly sliced
  • Dipping sauce: 2 tablespoons soy sauce mixed with 1 tablespoon vinegar (optional)

Instructions

  1. Put the flour in a bowl. Stir in the boiling water with a fork, then add the cold water and mix until shaggy. Knead for 3 to 4 minutes into a smooth, soft dough. Cover and rest for 15 minutes.
  2. Mix 2 tablespoons oil, the sesame oil and the salt in a small bowl.
  3. Divide the dough into 4 pieces. Roll one out into a thin rectangle, brush with the oil mixture and sprinkle with green onions.
  4. Roll it up from the long side into a rope, then coil the rope into a snail shape and tuck the end underneath. Gently roll it flat into a 7-inch pancake. Repeat with the rest.
  5. Heat 1 tablespoon oil in a skillet over medium heat. Cook each pancake for 2 to 3 minutes per side until golden and crisp, adding more oil as needed.
  6. Cut into wedges and serve with the soy-vinegar dipping sauce.

Pantry swaps

  • No sesame oil? Use all regular oil. You'll lose a little toasty flavor.
  • Make ahead? Freeze the uncooked pancakes between sheets of parchment and cook from frozen, adding a minute per side.

Rusty Chef tip: Boiling water makes the dough soft and easy to roll thin, so don't skip it.

Storage

Best eaten fresh. Uncooked pancakes freeze for up to 2 months.
