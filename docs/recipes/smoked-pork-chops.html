Smoked Pork Chops

By Daniel Carder · Dinner · 100 minutes

Jump to recipe

Low heat and a little wood smoke turn ordinary pork chops into something that tastes like it came from a barbecue joint. A simple brown sugar and paprika rub builds a sweet, smoky crust while the inside stays tender.

The smoker does almost all the work, and every spice in the rub is probably already in your cupboard.

From Daniel's kitchen ♥

When I have the smoker going for the weekend, I throw a few thick chops on too. They take about an hour and a half, and they come out juicy with real smoky flavor.– Daniel

Buy thick chops

Thin chops overcook before they pick up much smoke flavor, so ask for chops at least 1 1/4 inches thick. Pull them at 140°F; they finish at a safe 145°F while they rest, still juicy and slightly pink.

Want them extra juicy? Brine first

Stir 1/4 cup salt and 1/4 cup brown sugar into 4 cups of water until dissolved. Soak the chops in the fridge for 1 to 4 hours, then rinse, pat dry and cut the salt in the rub to 1/2 teaspoon.

Smoked Pork Chops

  • Prep10 min
  • Cook90 min
  • Total100 min
  • Serves4
  • Est. cost$2.60/serving

Ingredients

  • 4 thick-cut bone-in pork chops, 1 1/4 to 1 1/2 inches thick
  • 1 tablespoon brown sugar
  • 2 teaspoons paprika
  • 1 1/2 teaspoons salt
  • 1 teaspoon garlic powder
  • 1/2 teaspoon chili powder
  • 1 tablespoon oil
  • 2 tablespoons honey, for glazing (optional)

Instructions

  1. Heat your smoker to 225°F with hickory, apple or cherry wood.
  2. Pat the pork chops dry and rub them lightly with the oil.
  3. Mix the brown sugar, paprika, salt, garlic powder and chili powder, then coat both sides of the chops.
  4. Put the chops on the smoker grate and smoke for 60 to 90 minutes, until a thermometer in the thickest part reads 140°F.
  5. For a crisp crust, sear the chops in a very hot skillet or over a hot grill for 1 minute per side. Brush with honey during the last minute if you like.
  6. Rest the chops for 5 minutes (they will climb to 145°F), then serve.

Pantry swaps

  • No smoker? Set up a charcoal or gas grill for indirect heat (coals or burners on one side only), add a foil pouch of soaked wood chips and cook the chops on the cool side with the lid closed.
  • Boneless chops? They work too, but start checking the temperature after 45 minutes.
  • No brown sugar? White sugar or a drizzle of honey works.

Rusty Chef tip: Don't keep opening the smoker to check. Every peek lets heat and smoke escape and adds time. A probe thermometer you can read from outside is a great help.

What to serve with it

Try them with loaded baked potatoes, skillet cornbread or creamy mashed potatoes.

Storage

Leftovers keep 3 to 4 days in the fridge. Slice and warm gently in a covered pan with a splash of water, or chop the meat for sandwiches and fried rice.
