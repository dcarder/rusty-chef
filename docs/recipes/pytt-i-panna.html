Swedish Pytt i Panna (Potato Hash)

By Daniel Carder · Dinner · 40 minutes · Swedish

Jump to recipe

Pytt i panna means "small pieces in a pan," and it's the Swedish way to turn leftovers into a hearty dinner.

Crispy potatoes, onions and meat are fried together and topped with a fried egg and pickled beets.

Cook the potatoes first

Potatoes take the longest, so get them golden before adding the onion and meat. Small, even cubes cook faster and brown better.

Swedish Pytt i Panna (Potato Hash)

  • Prep15 min
  • Cook25 min
  • Total40 min
  • Serves4
  • Est. cost$1.40/serving

Ingredients

  • 2 lbs potatoes, cut into 1/2-inch cubes
  • 3 tablespoons oil
  • 2 tablespoons butter
  • 1 onion, diced
  • 2 cups diced cooked ham, sausage or leftover roast
  • Salt and pepper
  • 4 large eggs
  • 2 tablespoons chopped parsley (optional)
  • Pickled beets, for serving (optional)

Instructions

  1. Heat the oil in a large skillet over medium-high heat. Add the potatoes in a single layer and cook for about 15 minutes, turning now and then, until golden and tender.
  2. Add the butter and onion and cook for 5 minutes.
  3. Add the ham and cook for 5 minutes more until browned. Season with salt and pepper.
  4. Move the hash to plates and fry the eggs sunny-side up in the same pan.
  5. Top each plate with an egg and parsley, and serve with pickled beets.

Pantry swaps

  • Have leftover boiled potatoes? Use them and cut the potato cooking time in half.
  • No ham? Use any cooked sausage, hot dogs or leftover steak.

Rusty Chef tip: Don't stir the potatoes too often. Leaving them alone is how they get a crispy crust.

Storage

Keeps for 3 days in the fridge. Reheat in a skillet and fry fresh eggs.
