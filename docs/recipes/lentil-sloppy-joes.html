Lentil Sloppy Joes

By Daniel Carder · Dinner · 35 minutes

Jump to recipe

Lentils soak up sweet, tangy sloppy joe sauce just like ground beef, at a fraction of the price. They're also packed with protein and fiber.

Red or brown lentils both work. This recipe makes enough for four sandwiches or several lunches.

From Daniel's kitchen ♥

I'll be honest: I made these the first time because ground beef was too expensive that week. I didn't tell my wife or the kids they were lentils, and nobody noticed. Now it's a regular, cheap dinner that stretches into lunches for a couple of days.– Daniel

Which lentils to use

Brown or green lentils hold their shape and give a meatier texture. Red lentils cook faster and break down into a softer, saucier filling. Either way, there's no need to soak them.

Lentil Sloppy Joes

  • Prep5 min
  • Cook30 min
  • Total35 min
  • Serves4
  • Est. cost$1.06/serving

Ingredients

  • 1 tablespoon oil
  • 1 onion, diced
  • 1 cup dried lentils, rinsed
  • 1 can (14 oz) tomato sauce or crushed tomatoes
  • 2 tablespoons ketchup
  • 1 tablespoon mustard
  • 1 tablespoon brown sugar
  • 4 burger buns or bread rolls

Instructions

  1. Heat the oil in a pot and cook the onion for 5 minutes.
  2. Add the lentils and 2 1/2 cups water. Simmer for 20 to 25 minutes until tender, adding water if needed.
  3. Stir in the tomato sauce, ketchup, mustard and sugar. Simmer for 5 minutes until thick.
  4. Season with salt and pepper.
  5. Spoon onto toasted buns and serve.

Pantry swaps

  • No dried lentils? Use 2 cans of cooked lentils and skip step 2.
  • No buns? Serve over rice, baked potatoes or toast.
  • No mustard? A splash of vinegar or Worcestershire sauce.

Rusty Chef tip: Toast the buns in a dry skillet before filling. It stops them going soggy and adds crunch.

Storage

The filling keeps 5 days in the fridge and freezes well.
