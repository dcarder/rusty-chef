Easy Key Lime Pie

By Daniel Carder · Dessert · 40 minutes plus chilling · American

Jump to recipe

Key lime pie is a Florida classic, and the filling is just sweetened condensed milk, egg yolks and lime juice.

Whisk it together, bake it for 15 minutes and let the fridge do the rest.

Bottled juice is fine

Fresh key limes are tiny and take a lot of squeezing. Bottled key lime juice works great and is what a lot of Florida bakeries use. Regular lime juice works too, with a slightly sharper flavor.

Easy Key Lime Pie

  • Prep15 min
  • Bake25 min
  • Chill3 hr
  • Serves8
  • Est. cost$1.25/serving

Ingredients

  • 1 1/2 cups graham cracker crumbs (about 10 full sheets)
  • 1/3 cup sugar
  • 6 tablespoons butter, melted
  • 2 cans (14 oz each) sweetened condensed milk
  • 4 large egg yolks
  • 2/3 cup key lime juice or regular lime juice
  • 1 tablespoon lime zest (optional)
  • Whipped cream, for topping (optional)

Instructions

  1. Heat the oven to 350°F. Stir the graham cracker crumbs, sugar and melted butter together and press firmly into the bottom and up the sides of a 9-inch pie plate.
  2. Bake the crust for 10 minutes, then let it cool for a few minutes.
  3. Whisk the egg yolks and lime zest together for 1 minute. Whisk in the condensed milk, then the lime juice, until smooth and slightly thickened.
  4. Pour the filling into the crust. Bake for 15 minutes, until the edges are set and the center has a slight jiggle.
  5. Cool on the counter for 30 minutes, then refrigerate for at least 3 hours until cold and firm.
  6. Top with whipped cream and a little lime zest before serving.

Swaps and toppings

  • No graham crackers? Use vanilla wafers or a store-bought graham crust.
  • Leftover egg whites? Save them for an omelette or scrambled eggs the next morning.

Rusty Chef tip: Don't skip the chill time. The filling firms up in the fridge, and a cold pie slices much more cleanly.

Storage

Keeps for 4 days in the fridge, covered. Freeze for up to 1 month and thaw in the fridge.
