Kladdkaka (Swedish Sticky Chocolate Cake)

By Daniel Carder · Dessert · 28 minutes · Swedish

Jump to recipe

Kladdkaka means "sticky cake" in Swedish, and it's one of the most popular desserts in Sweden.

It's somewhere between a brownie and a molten chocolate cake, and it uses only basic pantry ingredients.

Underbake it on purpose

The center should still be gooey when it comes out of the oven. It firms up as it cools into a dense, fudgy middle.

Kladdkaka (Swedish Sticky Chocolate Cake)

  • Prep10 min
  • Cook18 min
  • Total28 min
  • Serves8
  • Est. cost$0.40/serving

Ingredients

  • 1/2 cup (1 stick) butter
  • 1 cup sugar
  • 2 large eggs
  • 1 teaspoon vanilla extract
  • 3/4 cup flour
  • 1/3 cup cocoa powder
  • 1/4 teaspoon salt
  • Powdered sugar and whipped cream, for serving (optional)

Instructions

  1. Heat the oven to 350°F. Grease an 8- or 9-inch round pan and dust it with cocoa powder or breadcrumbs, the Swedish way.
  2. Melt the butter in a saucepan and take it off the heat.
  3. Stir in the sugar, then the eggs and vanilla.
  4. Stir in the flour, cocoa and salt just until combined, and spread the batter in the pan.
  5. Bake for 15 to 18 minutes, until the edges are set but the center is still soft and wobbly.
  6. Let it cool in the pan for 30 minutes. Dust with powdered sugar and serve with whipped cream or berries.

Pantry swaps

  • No round pan? Use an 8-inch square pan.
  • Extra chocolatey? Stir in 1/2 cup chocolate chips with the flour.

Rusty Chef tip: Chill it for an hour before serving for clean slices and an extra-fudgy center.

Storage

Keeps for 2 days at room temperature or 4 days in the fridge.
