Pan-Seared Garlic Butter Pork Chops

By Daniel Carder · Dinner · 25 minutes

Jump to recipe

Pork chops get a bad reputation for being dry, but that is almost always from overcooking. A hot pan, a quick garlic-butter baste and a 5-minute rest give you a crisp, golden crust and a juicy center.

It all happens in one skillet, with seasonings you already have in the cupboard.

From Daniel's kitchen ♥

Pork chops are one of the best deals at the meat counter, and this is the way I cook them on a busy weeknight. Spooning that garlic butter over the top makes them taste like something from a steakhouse.– Daniel

Don't skip the thermometer

Pork is safe and still slightly pink at 145°F, followed by a short rest. Cooking it much past that is what makes chops tough and dry. Thin chops (under 3/4 inch) cook in about half the time, so start checking early.

Pan-Seared Garlic Butter Pork Chops

  • Prep5 min
  • Cook15 min
  • Total25 min
  • Serves4
  • Est. cost$2.10/serving

Ingredients

  • 4 bone-in pork chops, about 1 inch thick
  • 1 teaspoon salt
  • 1 teaspoon paprika
  • 1/2 teaspoon dried thyme or Italian seasoning
  • 1 tablespoon oil
  • 3 tablespoons butter
  • 4 cloves garlic, smashed
  • Chopped parsley (optional)

Instructions

  1. Take the pork chops out of the fridge 15 minutes before cooking and pat them very dry with paper towels.
  2. Mix the salt, paprika and dried herbs, then rub it all over both sides of the chops.
  3. Heat the oil in a large skillet over medium-high heat until it shimmers. Add the chops and cook without moving them for 4 to 5 minutes, until deep golden underneath.
  4. Flip the chops, turn the heat to medium and add the butter and garlic to the pan.
  5. As the butter melts, tilt the pan and spoon the foaming garlic butter over the chops for 3 to 5 minutes, until a thermometer in the thickest part reads 145°F.
  6. Move the chops to a plate and rest them for 5 minutes. Spoon the pan butter over the top, sprinkle with parsley and serve.

Pantry swaps

  • Boneless chops? They work too. Cook 3 to 4 minutes per side and check the temperature early.
  • No paprika? Use chili powder or just salt and pepper.
  • No fresh garlic? Add 1/2 teaspoon garlic powder to the spice rub instead.

Rusty Chef tip: If the chops have a thick fat cap, hold them on their edge with tongs for a minute at the start to render and crisp the fat.

What to serve with it

Try them with creamy mashed potatoes, crispy smashed potatoes or cabbage stir-fry.

Storage

Leftovers keep 3 to 4 days in the fridge. Slice thinly and warm gently in a covered pan with a splash of water so they don't dry out.
