Easy Sweet and Sour Chicken

By Daniel Carder · Dinner · 35 minutes · Chinese

Jump to recipe

That bright red takeout sauce is mostly ketchup, vinegar and sugar, so you probably have everything already.

An egg and cornstarch coating gives the chicken a light, crisp crust without a deep fryer.

Sauce it right before serving

The coating stays crispy longest if you toss the chicken in the sauce just before it goes on the table.

Easy Sweet and Sour Chicken

  • Prep15 min
  • Cook20 min
  • Total35 min
  • Serves4
  • Est. cost$2.00/serving

Ingredients

  • 1 1/2 lb boneless chicken breast, cut into 1-inch pieces
  • 2 large eggs, beaten
  • 1/2 cup cornstarch
  • 1/2 cup oil, for frying
  • 1 bell pepper, cut into chunks
  • 1/2 onion, cut into chunks
  • 1 can (8 oz) pineapple chunks, drained, juice saved (optional)
  • 1/3 cup ketchup
  • 1/3 cup vinegar
  • 1/2 cup sugar
  • 1 tablespoon soy sauce
  • Salt
  • Cooked rice, for serving (optional)

Instructions

  1. Stir together the ketchup, vinegar, sugar, soy sauce and 1/4 cup of the saved pineapple juice (or water). Set aside.
  2. Season the chicken with a pinch of salt. Dip the pieces in the beaten egg, then toss in the cornstarch until fully coated.
  3. Heat the oil in a large skillet over medium-high heat. Fry the chicken in batches for 3 to 4 minutes per side until golden and cooked through. Drain on paper towels.
  4. Pour off all but 1 tablespoon of oil. Add the pepper and onion and cook for 2 to 3 minutes until crisp-tender.
  5. Add the sauce and simmer for 2 minutes until slightly thickened. Add the chicken and pineapple, toss to coat and serve right away over rice.

Pantry swaps

  • No pineapple? Leave it out and use water in the sauce.
  • Want it lighter? Bake the coated chicken on a greased sheet pan at 425°F for 15 to 20 minutes, flipping once, or use an air fryer.

Rusty Chef tip: Don't crowd the pan when frying. If the pieces touch, they steam and lose their crunch.

Storage

Keeps for 3 days in the fridge. Reheat in the oven or air fryer to crisp it back up.
