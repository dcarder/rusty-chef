Banana Baked Oatmeal

By Daniel Carder · Breakfast · 45 minutes

Jump to recipe

Baked oatmeal is somewhere between a breakfast bar and a cake. Mix everything in one bowl, bake, and slice it into squares for the week.

It's a great use for overripe bananas sitting on the counter.

From Daniel's kitchen

Every time bananas start turning brown on the counter, I make this. It's like having cake for breakfast, and the kids grab a square on the way out the door. It's one of the easiest ways I've found to make breakfast ahead when the week gets hectic.

The riper the banana, the better

Bananas with lots of brown spots are sweeter and mash easily, so you can use less added sugar.

Banana Baked Oatmeal

  • Prep10 min
  • Cook35 min
  • Total45 min
  • Serves6

Ingredients

  • 2 ripe bananas, mashed
  • 2 cups rolled oats
  • 1 1/2 cups milk
  • 1 egg
  • 2 tablespoons honey
  • 1 teaspoon baking powder
  • 1 teaspoon cinnamon
  • Pinch of salt

Instructions

  1. Heat the oven to 375°F (190°C) and grease an 8x8-inch baking dish.
  2. Mash the bananas in a bowl, then whisk in the milk, egg and honey.
  3. Stir in the oats, baking powder, cinnamon and salt.
  4. Pour into the dish and bake for 30 to 35 minutes until set and golden.
  5. Cool for 10 minutes, then slice.

Pantry swaps

  • No bananas? Use 1 cup applesauce.
  • No egg? Use 1 extra mashed banana.

Rusty Chef tip: Add a handful of chocolate chips, raisins or chopped nuts before baking.

Storage

Keeps 5 days in the fridge. Reheat squares in the microwave for 30 seconds.

Easy Baked Ravioli (No-Boil)

By Daniel Carder · Dinner · 40 minutes

Jump to recipe

Baked ravioli tastes like lasagna but takes a fraction of the work. Frozen ravioli, a can of tomatoes and some cheese go into a dish and the oven does the rest.

There's no boiling: the ravioli cook right in the sauce. It's one of our most-made weeknight dinners and a guaranteed hit with kids.

From Daniel's kitchen

This is the number-one most requested dinner from my kids. It tastes like lasagna but takes five minutes to put together, because you don't even boil the ravioli. On nights when I'm overwhelmed, I throw it in the oven and know everybody will eat.

Why you don't need to boil the ravioli

Frozen ravioli are thin and partly cooked already. Covered with foil, the steam from the sauce cooks them through in the oven while they soak up flavor. Use enough sauce to cover every piece, or exposed edges turn hard and dry.

Easy Baked Ravioli (No-Boil)

  • Prep5 min
  • Cook35 min
  • Total40 min
  • Serves6

Ingredients

  • 1 bag (25 oz) frozen cheese ravioli
  • 1 can (28 oz) crushed tomatoes or 3 cups marinara
  • 2 cloves garlic, minced
  • 1 teaspoon dried Italian herbs
  • 1/2 teaspoon salt
  • 2 cups shredded mozzarella
  • 1/2 cup grated parmesan
  • 1 tablespoon olive oil (optional)
  • Chopped parsley or basil (optional)

Instructions

  1. Heat the oven to 400°F (200°C). Lightly oil a 9x13-inch baking dish.
  2. Stir the crushed tomatoes with the garlic, herbs and salt. (Skip this if using jarred marinara.)
  3. Spread a thin layer of sauce in the dish. Add half the frozen ravioli in a single layer, then half the remaining sauce and half the mozzarella.
  4. Repeat with the rest of the ravioli and sauce, making sure every piece is covered. Top with the remaining mozzarella and the parmesan.
  5. Cover tightly with foil and bake for 25 minutes. Uncover and bake 10 to 15 minutes more until bubbling and golden.
  6. Rest for 5 minutes, then top with herbs and serve.

Pantry swaps

  • No mozzarella? Any melting cheese, or dollops of ricotta or cottage cheese.
  • Want meat? Brown 1 lb ground beef or Italian sausage and stir it into the sauce.
  • Fresh ravioli? Reduce the covered baking time to 15 minutes.

Rusty Chef tip: Add a handful of spinach between the layers. It wilts into the sauce and nobody notices the vegetables.

Storage

Keeps 4 days in the fridge. Reheat covered at 350°F for 15 minutes, or microwave single portions.

Cheesy Bean Quesadillas

By Daniel Carder · Lunch · 15 minutes

Jump to recipe

Quesadillas are the fastest hot lunch in the pantry. Beans, cheese and tortillas, crisped in a pan.

Serve with salsa, sour cream or guacamole for dipping.

From Daniel's kitchen

When the kids are hungry and I've got nothing planned, these save the day. Beans, cheese and tortillas are always in my kitchen. They're crispy, cheesy and on the plate in about ten minutes, and nobody complains.

Medium heat is key

Too hot and the tortilla burns before the cheese melts. Medium heat gives a crisp, evenly golden outside and a gooey middle.

Cheesy Bean Quesadillas

  • Prep5 min
  • Cook10 min
  • Total15 min
  • Serves2

Ingredients

  • 4 tortillas
  • 1 cup canned beans, drained
  • 1 cup shredded cheese
  • 1/2 teaspoon cumin
  • 1/2 teaspoon chili powder (optional)
  • 1 teaspoon oil
  • Salsa (optional)

Instructions

  1. Mash the beans roughly with the cumin, chili powder and a pinch of salt.
  2. Spread beans over half of each tortilla and top with cheese. Fold over.
  3. Heat a lightly oiled skillet over medium heat.
  4. Cook for 2 to 3 minutes per side until golden and melted.
  5. Cut into wedges and serve with salsa.

Pantry swaps

  • No beans? Leftover rice, corn or shredded chicken.
  • No tortillas? Make a grilled cheese with the same filling.

Rusty Chef tip: Press down with a spatula after flipping to help the cheese glue everything together.

Storage

Best fresh. The bean filling keeps 4 days.

Smoky Black Bean Soup

By Daniel Carder · Soup · 25 minutes

Jump to recipe

This soup tastes like it simmered all day but takes about 25 minutes. Canned beans do the heavy lifting.

Blending part of the soup makes it creamy without any cream. Top with cheese, a dollop of yogurt or crushed tortilla chips.

From Daniel's kitchen

This soup has gotten me through more than a few lean weeks. Two cans of beans and some spices turn into a thick, hearty pot that feeds the whole family. Top it with a little cheese and crushed tortilla chips, and the kids are happy.

Blend half

Blend about half the soup and stir it back in. You get a creamy base with whole beans for texture.

Smoky Black Bean Soup

  • Prep5 min
  • Cook20 min
  • Total25 min
  • Serves4

Ingredients

  • 1 tablespoon oil
  • 1 onion, diced
  • 3 cloves garlic
  • 2 teaspoons cumin
  • 1 teaspoon chili powder
  • 2 cans (15 oz each) black beans, with liquid
  • 2 cups stock
  • Salt
  • Lime juice (optional)

Instructions

  1. Heat the oil and cook the onion for 5 minutes.
  2. Add garlic, cumin and chili powder and stir for 1 minute.
  3. Add the beans with their liquid and the stock. Simmer for 15 minutes.
  4. Blend half the soup and stir it back into the pot.
  5. Season with salt and a squeeze of lime.

Pantry swaps

  • No stock? Water plus 1/2 teaspoon extra salt.
  • No chili powder? Smoked paprika is even better.

Rusty Chef tip: A spoonful of salsa stirred in at the end adds instant brightness and flavor.

Storage

Keeps 5 days in the fridge or 3 months frozen.

Canned Black Bean Tacos

By Daniel Carder · Dinner · 15 minutes

Jump to recipe

A can of black beans and a few spices make a taco filling that's smoky, savory and filling. Kids love these, and they cost a fraction of takeout.

Pile on whatever toppings you have: cheese, salsa, a squeeze of lime, sliced onion or a spoonful of sour cream.

From Daniel's kitchen

Taco night doesn't have to mean buying a pound of meat. I started making these when I was stretching the grocery budget, and my kids didn't even notice the beef was missing. Set out the toppings and let everybody build their own. It cuts down on the picky-eater complaints.

Mash some, keep some whole

Mashing about a third of the beans with the back of a fork creates a creamy base that holds the rest together, so the filling doesn't roll out of the tortilla.

Canned Black Bean Tacos

  • Prep5 min
  • Cook10 min
  • Total15 min
  • Serves3

Ingredients

  • 1 tablespoon oil
  • 1/2 onion, diced
  • 1 can (15 oz) black beans, drained
  • 1 teaspoon cumin
  • 1 teaspoon chili powder
  • 6 small tortillas
  • Salt
  • Shredded cheese (optional)
  • Salsa (optional)

Instructions

  1. Heat the oil in a skillet over medium heat and cook the onion for 4 minutes.
  2. Add the cumin and chili powder and stir for 30 seconds.
  3. Add the beans and 3 tablespoons water. Simmer for 5 minutes, mashing about a third of the beans.
  4. Warm the tortillas in a dry pan or wrapped in a damp towel in the microwave.
  5. Fill the tortillas with beans and top with cheese and salsa.

Pantry swaps

  • No black beans? Pinto or kidney beans work the same way.
  • No tortillas? Serve over rice as a burrito bowl, or on toasted bread.
  • No chili powder? Use paprika plus a pinch of cayenne.

Rusty Chef tip: Char the tortillas directly over a gas flame for a few seconds per side. It adds a toasty flavor that makes them taste homemade.

Storage

The bean filling keeps 4 days in the fridge.

Freezer Breakfast Burritos

By Daniel Carder · Breakfast · 20 minutes

Jump to recipe

Breakfast burritos are the ultimate make-ahead breakfast. Roll a batch on the weekend and you've got a hot breakfast in two minutes all week.

This version uses eggs, canned beans and cheese, but it's a great home for leftover potatoes or vegetables too.

From Daniel's kitchen

I make a batch of these on Sunday afternoons, and it's like giving my future self a gift. On busy mornings, they go from freezer to microwave to out the door in two minutes. It's way cheaper than the drive-thru, and the kids actually eat them.

Undercook the eggs slightly

Take the eggs off the heat while still a little glossy. They'll finish cooking when you reheat the burrito and won't turn rubbery.

Freezer Breakfast Burritos

  • Prep10 min
  • Cook10 min
  • Total20 min
  • Serves4

Ingredients

  • 6 eggs
  • 1 can (15 oz) black beans, drained
  • 1 cup shredded cheese
  • 4 large tortillas
  • 1 tablespoon butter
  • Salt and pepper
  • Salsa (optional)

Instructions

  1. Warm the beans in a small pan with a pinch of salt.
  2. Whisk the eggs with salt and pepper. Scramble in butter over medium-low heat until just set.
  3. Divide eggs, beans and cheese between the tortillas and add salsa if using.
  4. Fold in the sides and roll up tightly.
  5. Eat right away, or wrap individually in foil and freeze.

Pantry swaps

  • No black beans? Pinto or refried beans.
  • No tortillas? Serve the filling over toast.

Rusty Chef tip: To reheat from frozen, remove the foil, wrap in a damp paper towel and microwave for 2 minutes, flipping halfway.

Storage

Freezes for up to 3 months.

Garlicky Cabbage Stir-Fry

By Daniel Carder · Side · 15 minutes

Jump to recipe

Cabbage is one of the cheapest vegetables you can buy and lasts weeks in the fridge.

Stir-fried hot and fast with garlic and soy sauce, it turns sweet and tender-crisp. Serve with rice or noodles, or top with a fried egg.

From Daniel's kitchen

A head of cabbage is one of the cheapest things in the produce section and it lasts forever in the fridge. Cooked hot and fast with garlic and soy sauce, it surprised my kids, who swore they hated cabbage. Now they ask for it with rice.

High heat, short time

Cabbage turns watery and limp if it cooks too slowly. A very hot pan gives charred edges and keeps some crunch.

Garlicky Cabbage Stir-Fry

  • Prep5 min
  • Cook10 min
  • Total15 min
  • Serves4

Ingredients

  • 1/2 head cabbage, thinly sliced
  • 2 tablespoons oil
  • 3 cloves garlic, sliced
  • 2 tablespoons soy sauce
  • 1 teaspoon vinegar
  • 1 teaspoon sugar (optional)
  • Chili flakes (optional)

Instructions

  1. Heat the oil in a wok or large skillet over high heat until shimmering.
  2. Add the garlic and chili flakes and stir for 15 seconds.
  3. Add the cabbage and stir-fry for 4 to 5 minutes until wilted with charred edges.
  4. Add the soy sauce, vinegar and sugar and toss for 30 seconds.
  5. Serve right away.

Pantry swaps

  • No soy sauce? Salt plus a splash of Worcestershire.
  • Want a meal? Add cooked noodles and a fried egg.

Rusty Chef tip: Cook in two batches if your pan is small. Crowding the pan steams the cabbage.

Storage

Keeps 3 days in the fridge.

10-Minute Pantry Fried Rice

By Daniel Carder · Dinner · 10 minutes

Jump to recipe

Fried rice is the ultimate pantry rescue meal. Got leftover rice, a couple of eggs and a bag of frozen vegetables? You've got dinner in ten minutes.

This is the recipe our robot chef falls back on when the fridge looks empty. Swap the vegetables, add leftover chicken or tofu, or turn up the heat with chili crisp. The method matters more than the exact ingredients.

From Daniel's kitchen

This is the meal that got me through more than one week when money was tight and the fridge looked empty. Leftover rice, a couple of eggs and a bag of frozen veggies, and my picky kids still ask for seconds. It's cheaper and faster than ordering takeout, and honestly they like mine better.

Why day-old rice works best

Freshly cooked rice is full of steam, so it clumps and turns mushy in the pan. Rice that has chilled overnight dries out slightly, so the grains separate and crisp at the edges. Only have fresh rice? Spread it on a plate and freeze it for 10 to 15 minutes first.

10-Minute Pantry Fried Rice

  • Prep3 min
  • Cook7 min
  • Total10 min
  • Serves2

Ingredients

  • 2 cups cooked rice, preferably a day old
  • 2 large eggs, beaten
  • 1 cup frozen mixed vegetables
  • 2 tablespoons neutral oil
  • 2 cloves garlic, minced (or 1/2 teaspoon garlic powder)
  • 2 tablespoons soy sauce
  • 1 teaspoon sesame oil (optional)
  • 2 green onions, sliced (optional)

Instructions

  1. Heat 1 tablespoon oil in a large skillet or wok over high heat. Scramble the eggs until just set, then move them to a plate.
  2. Add the remaining oil, the frozen vegetables and the garlic. Stir-fry for about 2 minutes until hot.
  3. Add the rice and break up any clumps. Spread it out and let it sit for 1 minute to crisp, then stir. Repeat once.
  4. Pour the soy sauce around the edge of the pan so it sizzles, return the eggs and toss everything together.
  5. Turn off the heat, drizzle with sesame oil and top with green onions.

Pantry swaps

  • No soy sauce? Use tamari, coconut aminos, or 1 tablespoon Worcestershire sauce with a pinch of salt.
  • No frozen veg? Use diced onion, drained canned corn, chopped cabbage or any fresh vegetables you need to use up.
  • No eggs? Add crumbled firm tofu or a handful of canned chickpeas.

Rusty Chef tip: Keep the heat high and don't overcrowd the pan. If you double the recipe, cook it in two batches or the rice will steam instead of fry.

Storage

Keeps 3 days in the fridge. Reheat in a hot skillet with a splash of oil to bring back the crispy bits.

Pasta Aglio e Olio

By Daniel Carder · Dinner · 17 minutes

Jump to recipe

Aglio e olio means garlic and oil, and that's really all this classic Italian pasta needs. It's what cooks in Naples make late at night when the cupboards are bare.

Because there are so few ingredients, technique matters: gently golden garlic and starchy pasta water turn plain oil into a silky sauce.

From Daniel's kitchen

When it's just me or two of us, this is my go-to. It costs almost nothing, it's ready before you could even decide what to order, and it tastes like something from a nice Italian place. I make it when I'm tired and want something that still feels like a treat.

The secret is pasta water

Starchy pasta water is the glue that turns oil into sauce. Tossing pasta with oil and a splash of that cloudy water emulsifies them, so the sauce clings instead of pooling at the bottom of the bowl. Always save a mugful before draining.

Pasta Aglio e Olio

  • Prep5 min
  • Cook12 min
  • Total17 min
  • Serves2

Ingredients

  • 8 oz spaghetti
  • 1/3 cup olive oil
  • 5 cloves garlic, thinly sliced
  • 1/2 teaspoon red pepper flakes
  • Salt
  • Grated parmesan (optional)
  • Chopped parsley (optional)

Instructions

  1. Cook the spaghetti in well-salted boiling water until just al dente. Save 1 cup of pasta water, then drain.
  2. While the pasta cooks, warm the olive oil and garlic in a large skillet over medium-low heat. Cook gently until the garlic is pale golden, 3 to 4 minutes.
  3. Add the red pepper flakes and cook for 15 seconds.
  4. Add the pasta and 1/2 cup pasta water. Toss vigorously for 1 to 2 minutes until glossy, adding more water if it looks dry.
  5. Season with salt and finish with parmesan and parsley if you have them.

Spaghetti With 20-Minute Marinara

By Daniel Carder · Dinner · 25 minutes

Jump to recipe

Homemade marinara takes barely longer than heating up a jar, and it tastes far better.

It's just tomatoes, garlic, olive oil and herbs. Make a double batch and freeze half.

From Daniel's kitchen

Spaghetti night is a staple in our house because it's cheap, easy and everybody eats it. Once I learned how simple homemade sauce is, I stopped buying jars. I make a double batch and freeze half for a night when I'm too tired to cook.

Crush whole tomatoes by hand

Whole canned tomatoes are often better quality than pre-crushed. Squeeze them through your fingers into the pan for a rustic, chunky sauce.

Spaghetti With 20-Minute Marinara

  • Prep5 min
  • Cook20 min
  • Total25 min
  • Serves4

Ingredients

  • 1 lb spaghetti
  • 3 tablespoons olive oil
  • 4 cloves garlic, sliced
  • 1 can (28 oz) whole tomatoes
  • 1 teaspoon dried Italian herbs
  • Pinch of sugar (optional)
  • Salt
  • Grated parmesan (optional)

Instructions

  1. Warm the oil and garlic in a large pan over medium-low heat for 2 minutes.
  2. Add the tomatoes, crushing them by hand, plus the herbs and a pinch of salt.
  3. Simmer for 15 to 20 minutes until thickened. Add sugar if it tastes sharp.
  4. Meanwhile, cook the spaghetti. Save a little water.
  5. Toss the pasta with the sauce and a splash of pasta water. Serve with parmesan.

Pantry swaps

  • No whole tomatoes? Crushed or diced work fine.
  • No dried herbs? A pinch of oregano or chili flakes.

Rusty Chef tip: Finish the pasta in the sauce for the last minute instead of spooning sauce on top. It absorbs the flavor.

Easy Spanish Rice

By Daniel Carder · Side · 30 minutes

Jump to recipe

Also called Mexican rice or arroz rojo, this restaurant-style side is easy to make at home.

Toasting the rice before adding liquid gives it a nutty flavor and keeps the grains separate.

From Daniel's kitchen

This is the rice I make anytime we have tacos or enchiladas. It's the kind of side you'd get at a Mexican restaurant here in Florida, but made with a little tomato and pantry spices. Once you toast the rice first, you'll never go back to plain.

Toast the rice first

Frying the dry rice in oil until lightly golden coats each grain so it cooks up fluffy, not sticky.

Easy Spanish Rice

  • Prep5 min
  • Cook25 min
  • Total30 min
  • Serves4

Ingredients

  • 1 cup long-grain rice
  • 2 tablespoons oil
  • 1/2 onion, diced
  • 2 cloves garlic
  • 1/2 cup canned tomato sauce or crushed tomatoes
  • 1 3/4 cups stock
  • 1/2 teaspoon cumin
  • Salt

Instructions

  1. Heat the oil and toast the rice, stirring, for 4 to 5 minutes until lightly golden.
  2. Add the onion and garlic and cook for 2 minutes.
  3. Stir in the tomato, stock, cumin and salt. Bring to a boil.
  4. Cover, reduce to low and cook for 18 minutes without lifting the lid.
  5. Rest for 5 minutes, then fluff with a fork.

Pantry swaps

  • No stock? Water with extra salt.
  • No tomato sauce? Blend a few canned tomatoes.

Rusty Chef tip: Resist peeking. Lifting the lid lets out the steam the rice needs to cook evenly.

Storage

Keeps 4 days. Reheat with a splash of water.

From Daniel's kitchen

Cut small, even pieces

Sweet Potato & Black Bean Skillet

Ingredients

  5. Season and finish with lime juice.

Tuscan White Bean & Garlic Soup

By Daniel Carder · Soup · 25 minutes

Inspired by Tuscan peasant cooking, this soup turns a couple of cans of white beans into something rich and elegant.

This feels like something you'd order at a nice Italian restaurant, but it's two cans of beans and a lot of garlic. When it's just me, I make a pot and eat it for a few days with crusty bread. It never gets old.

Tuscan White Bean & Garlic Soup

  • Prep5 min

Kitchen Essentials

The tools and pantry staples that make every recipe on The Rusty Chef possible.

You don't need a fancy kitchen to cook well. These are the pieces of equipment we reach for again and again, and the staples worth keeping stocked so you can always pull together a meal.

Tools we actually use

A heavy 10- or 12-inch skillet

Cast iron or a heavy stainless steel pan does most of the work on this site: fried rice, hash, quesadillas, frittatas and cornbread. Heavy pans hold heat, so food browns instead of steaming.

A large pot with a lid

For pasta, soups, chili and rice. A 5- to 6-quart pot is big enough for a full batch of soup without boiling over.

A sharp chef's knife

A sharp knife is safer than a dull one because it doesn't slip. You don't need an expensive set; one good 8-inch knife covers almost everything.

A sturdy cutting board

Choose wood or thick plastic, big enough that ingredients don't fall off the edge. Put a damp towel underneath so it doesn't slide.

A 9x13-inch baking dish

Essential for baked ravioli, baked oatmeal and casseroles. Glass or ceramic both work.

A sheet pan

For roasted chickpeas, biscuits, cookies and roasting vegetables. A rimmed pan stops juices from dripping into the oven.

Measuring cups and spoons

Cooking is forgiving, but baking isn't. Accurate measuring is the difference between fluffy pancakes and flat ones.

An immersion (stick) blender

Blends soups right in the pot, so you don't have to pour hot liquid into a blender. Also great for hummus and sauces.

A fine-mesh sieve

For rinsing rice, lentils and canned beans, and for draining pasta when you want to save the cooking water.

Airtight containers

Most of our recipes make great leftovers. Good containers keep food fresh longer and make meal prep easy.
